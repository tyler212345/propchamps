What Is a Daily Loss Limit? Every Futures Firm's DLL

A daily loss limit (DLL) is the most you can lose in one session before the firm stops you or fails the account, and it's separate from your max drawdown. Here is how it works, what happens when you hit it, and every futures firm's DLL by size.

A daily loss limit (DLL) is the most you're allowed to lose in a single day before a prop firm stops you for the day or fails the account — even when you still have plenty of overall drawdown left. It's one of the most misunderstood rules in futures prop trading, and it differs by firm, by plan and by stage. Here's how it works and what every firm we track sets it at.

A daily loss limit and a maximum drawdown are not the same thing, and confusing them is how a lot of funded accounts quietly die. Your max drawdown (or max loss limit) is the total you can be down from your peak before you're out. The daily loss limit is a separate, tighter ceiling that resets every day — blow past it in one bad session and you're stopped for the day, or failed outright if the limit is a hard one, even with room to spare on your overall drawdown.

How a daily loss limit works

Say you're on a $50,000 account with a $2,000 max drawdown and a $1,000 daily loss limit. You start the day flat. A bad morning drags you down $1,050 — and you're done for the day, or out entirely if the DLL is hard. Not because you broke the $2,000 total drawdown (you were only down half of it), but because you crossed the $1,000 daily ceiling.

That's the trap: the DLL is tighter than your overall risk, and it resets each day, so it's an easy rule to break on a single volatile session — a news spike, a revenge trade, a stop that slips. Many traders fail on the DLL while their account still had thousands of dollars of "real" room left.

Daily loss limit vs. max drawdown

  • Max drawdown — your total allowable loss from the account's peak balance. Cross it and the account is done. It's the rule that defines your overall risk.
  • Daily loss limit — a per-day cap that resets at the session boundary. It exists to stop a single catastrophic day, and it's usually well below your total drawdown.
  • Trailing vs. static — a max drawdown often trails your peak (it moves up as you profit), while a DLL is a flat daily number. Our drawdown explainer breaks the trailing mechanics down in detail.

What does "daily loss limit below initial trail" mean? (Lucid LucidScale)

It's Lucid's wording for the fixed DLL that applies while your funded balance is still below the initial trail level. Once your balance is above that level, LucidScale replaces the fixed figure with a scaling one: 60% of your highest end-of-day profit, per Lucid's help center. In practice the limit grows as your best closing balance grows, so a good run buys more daily room, not less.

Lucid's DLL is optional on Pro, Flex and Daily (you choose at purchase), and Direct carries one from 50K up. Keeping it on has a payout upside: funded Pro, Flex and Daily accounts with the DLL switched on (and 50K+ Direct) can request a payout during the session from that session's profit. Confirm the live figure in your Lucid dashboard before you trade near it.

Soft vs hard DLL: what happens when you hit it

A soft DLL stops you for the rest of the session: the account survives and you trade again tomorrow. A hard DLL fails the account the moment you cross it. The firms word theirs differently:

  • Apex Trader Funding: per Apex's help center, hitting the daily loss limit ends your trading day, while touching the trailing threshold ends the account.
  • Top One Futures: the funded Elite Access DLL and the Ignite DLL are soft limits.
  • My Funded Futures: Builder's limit is a soft pause, not a breach.
  • Everyone else: read the exact wording in your firm's rules before you lean on it, because a blown DLL can cost the account.

Is no daily loss limit actually better?

For most active traders, yes — it removes the tightest and most arbitrary way to fail, and it lets you recover within a session instead of being stopped out on a bad hour. It's especially valuable if you trade the open, hold through news, or scale contracts.

But "no DLL" is not "no rules." You still have to respect your max drawdown, and some no-DLL firms pair the missing daily limit with a tighter trailing drawdown that can be just as punishing if you give back profit. Read the drawdown method alongside the DLL — the two rules work together, and a firm can loosen one while quietly tightening the other. Compare the full rule set before you decide.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What is a daily loss limit in prop trading?+

A daily loss limit is the maximum you can lose in a single trading day before the firm closes or pauses your account. It resets each day and sits below your total max drawdown, so it's possible to break it while still having overall room left.

What's the difference between a daily loss limit and a max drawdown?+

The max drawdown is your total allowable loss from the account's peak — cross it and you're done for good. The daily loss limit is a smaller per-day ceiling that resets every session. You can fail either one independently.

Is there a daily loss limit on Apex?+

It depends on the account type. Apex's EOD Trail account has a DLL of $500 / $1,000 / $1,500 / $2,000 (25K–150K). The Intraday Trail evaluation has none, but once you're funded the Intraday Performance Account adds a DLL that scales with your tier. Hitting the DLL ends your trading day; touching the trailing threshold ends the account. More in our Apex drawdown guide.

Is there a daily loss limit on Alpha Futures?+

On Zero and Direct, yes: $500 / $1,000 / $2,000 on Zero (25K–100K) and $500 / $1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000 on Direct (25K–150K). Standard has none in the evaluation and $1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000 once qualified. Advanced has no daily loss limit at any stage.

Is it better to trade without a daily loss limit?+

For most active traders, yes — it removes the most common surprise failure and lets you recover intraday. Just remember you still have to respect the account's max drawdown, which is the rule that ultimately defines your risk.

Can you still fail a no-DLL account?+

Absolutely. You can still breach the max drawdown, violate a consistency rule, or break firm-specific rules like news restrictions. No daily loss limit removes one failure mode, not all of them.

Does the daily loss limit reset every day?+

Yes. A daily loss limit is measured per session and resets at the session boundary, unlike the max drawdown, which never resets. Most DLLs are a flat dollar figure; Lucid's LucidScale is the exception, scaling with your highest end-of-day profit once you're above the initial trail.
