Prop Firm Payout Caps: The Max Payout at Every Futures Firm

Take Profit Trader's PRO account has no maximum withdrawal, Lucid Daily and MFF Rapid EOD have no per-payout cap, and Alpha Advanced allows $15,000 per request. Most other plans cap each payout at $500 to $6,000. Here is every cap, size by size.

The short version
  • A payout cap limits each withdrawal request, not your total earnings. Profit above the cap generally waits for your next request, unless the plan has a hard profit limit.
  • No per-request cap: Take Profit Trader PRO (no maximum withdrawal), Lucid Daily and MFF Rapid EOD. MFF Rapid states no per-payout cap either, which isn't the same as a confirmed "no cap".
  • Highest fixed cap: Alpha Futures Advanced at $15,000 per request on every size, then FundedNext Legacy at $6,000 (50K and 100K), then $5,000 on a 150K at Alpha Standard and, once their ladders top out, Apex and Tradeify Growth (per their help centers as indexed).
  • Caps come in three styles: ladders that rise with each payout (Apex, Tradeify Growth and Lightning, Lucid Pro and Direct), flat caps by size, and "50% of profit" caps (Alpha Zero, Standard and Advanced, Lucid Flex, Tradeify Select Flex, FundedNext Flex and Legacy).
  • A cap can make a 90% split pay less per request than an 80% split with no cap; the worked example below shows the math.

A payout cap is the most you can withdraw in a single request, and it often matters more than the profit split. Among the 27 futures plans we track, Take Profit Trader's PRO account has no maximum at all, Lucid Daily and MFF's Rapid EOD have no per-payout cap, and Alpha's Advanced plan allows $15,000 per request. Most others cap each payout somewhere between $500 and $6,000, sized to the account. Here is every plan's cap, size by size, and how to read them.

What is a payout cap?

A payout cap is the maximum a funded account lets you withdraw in one payout request. It's separate from the other gates on a payout: the minimum request (the least you can ask for), the buffer (profit you must leave in the account) and the consistency rule (how evenly the profit has to be spread). You can clear all of those and still be limited by the cap.

Most caps scale with the account size, so a 150K allows more per request than a 25K. Many also rise over your first few payouts, which is why firms call them ladders.

Which futures prop firms have no payout cap?

These are the plans with no per-request maximum in their published rules:

  • Take Profit Trader (PRO): no maximum withdrawal, no payout windows and no funded consistency rule. You can withdraw from day one once your balance clears a buffer equal to the max drawdown, which means a balance of $26,500 / $52,000 / $77,500 / $103,000 / $154,500 on the 25K to 150K accounts. PRO+ is the live stage above it.
  • Lucid Daily: no per-payout cap: you can request the full sim profit above the buffer, every day. The limit is elsewhere: a maximum daily sim profit of $6,000 / $8,000 / $10,000 / $12,000, and reaching it moves you to a live account automatically.
  • My Funded Futures Rapid EOD (25K, 50K): "no cap" per payout, daily, once you've cleared the $1,100 / $2,100 buffer and then $250 / $500 of new profit per payout.
  • My Funded Futures Rapid: no per-payout cap is stated, which is not quite the same as a confirmed "no cap". A $10,000 net profit day moves you to Live automatically, and profit above $10K that day is forfeited.

Two more come close. MFF Pro caps sim payouts at $100,000 per user in total rather than per request, and FundedNext Legacy removes both its cap and its 50%-of-profit limit after 30 benchmark days.

The three kinds of payout cap

Ladders start low and rise with each payout you take. Apex's 150K goes from $2,500 on the first payout to $5,000 by the sixth; Tradeify Growth reaches its top cap from the fourth payout; Lucid Pro rises after the first, and Lucid Direct and Tradeify Lightning after the third. The first payouts are the tightest, which is the firm testing you before it lets more out. The Apex and Tradeify ladders are per their help centers as indexed in October 2026, so confirm them in your dashboard.

Flat caps stay the same on every request and only change with account size: Top One's plans, Alpha Direct, MFF Builder and FundedNext Rapid all work this way.

"50% of profit" caps limit each request to half the profit in the account, up to a dollar maximum. Alpha Zero, Standard and Advanced; Lucid Flex; Tradeify Select Flex; and FundedNext Flex and Legacy use this. On a 50K Alpha Standard with $4,000 of profit, for example, half is $2,000, so that's the most you can request even though the dollar cap is $3,000.

Can a payout cap make a 90% split pay less than 80%?

Yes, per request. Take two 50K accounts, each with $6,000 of profit you're allowed to withdraw:

  • Account A pays 90% but caps each request at $2,000. If the cap applies to the gross amount (as at Alpha, where the request is the amount taken off the account and you receive 90% of it), your first payout is $1,800.
  • Account B pays 80% with no per-request cap. Withdraw the full $6,000 and you receive $4,800.

Account A pays the better split and still puts less in your pocket per request; it takes three capped requests to move the same $6,000, at whatever cadence the plan allows. This is an illustration, not a quote for any one firm. The real comparison also depends on how often you can request, the buffer each plan makes you leave in, and whether the plan ends after a set number of payouts.

Caps, buffers and minimums: what else limits a payout?

A cap is the ceiling on a request; three other rules decide whether you can make one at all:

  • The buffer: profit that has to stay in the account. At Take Profit Trader it equals the max drawdown; on Lucid Pro it's the initial max loss limit plus $100; Top One's Elite Daily keeps $1,500 to $4,500 in the account.
  • The minimum request: from $200 (Alpha Zero) to $1,000 (Alpha Advanced, MFF Pro, Tradeify Lightning), or $1,500 on a 150K Tradeify Growth or MFF Builder.
  • The consistency rule: how evenly your profit has to be spread before it's withdrawable. See consistency rules by firm.

The full sequence, from qualifying days to processing time, is in how prop firm payouts work.

Do payout caps ever go away?

Often, yes, just not on day one:

  • Ladders top out. Once you reach the final rung (Apex's 6th payout, Tradeify Growth's 4th, per their help centers as indexed) the cap stops rising, but it doesn't disappear.
  • Some plans drop the cap. FundedNext Legacy removes its cap after 30 benchmark days.
  • Live accounts work differently. Top One caps every sim-funded withdrawal until you go live. Alpha's Live Program allows daily, uncapped withdrawals at an 80% split. Lucid moves funded traders to live after their payouts (Flex after 5).
  • Some plans end instead. Apex closes a performance account after 6 payouts; FundedNext Flex and Rapid end after 5 rewards; Lucid Flex moves you live after 5.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Which futures prop firm has no payout cap?+

Take Profit Trader's PRO account has no maximum withdrawal: once your balance clears a buffer equal to the max drawdown, you can withdraw daily with no cap. Lucid Daily and My Funded Futures' Rapid EOD also have no per-payout cap, and MFF Rapid states none. Each has another limit instead, such as Lucid Daily's maximum daily sim profit or MFF Rapid's $10,000-day move to Live.

Which prop firm has the highest payout cap?+

Among fixed per-request caps, Alpha Futures' Advanced plan is the highest we track at $15,000 per request on every size. Next is FundedNext Legacy at $6,000 on the 50K and 100K, then $5,000 caps on the 150K accounts at Alpha Standard (flat), Apex (by the 6th payout) and Tradeify Growth (from the 4th payout). The Apex and Tradeify figures come from search-index copies of their help centers, which blocked our Oct 23, 2026 check, so confirm them in your dashboard.

What is a payout cap in prop trading?+

It's the most you can withdraw in one payout request from a funded account. It's set by account size and often rises over your first few payouts. It sits alongside the minimum request, the buffer and any consistency rule, which decide whether you can request at all.

Does the payout cap include the profit split?+

It depends on the firm's wording. At Alpha Futures the request is the amount taken off the account and you receive 90% of it, so a $2,000 request pays you $1,800. Other firms don't say as clearly, so check your firm's payout page before you plan around the number.

What happens to profit above the cap?+

Under a per-request cap, it generally stays in the account for your next request; the cap limits how much moves per payout, not how much you can earn. The exceptions are hard profit limits: profit above MFF Pro's $100,000 per-user cap is forfeited, MFF Rapid forfeits profit above $10,000 in a single day, and Lucid Daily's maximum daily sim profit moves you to live.

Is a firm with no payout cap always better?+

No. A no-cap plan can still pay less overall if it has a lower split, a larger buffer or a monthly fee. Take Profit Trader's PRO account, for example, pays 80/20 and bills monthly until you pass. Compare the cap together with the split, the cadence and the cost.
