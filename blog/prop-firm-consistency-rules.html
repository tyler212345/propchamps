Prop Firm Consistency Rule Explained: Every Firm's Number

A consistency rule caps how much of your profit can come from your best day: best day ÷ total profit must stay under the firm's percentage, from 15% to 50%. Here is how it's calculated, when it applies, and every futures firm's rule, plan by plan.

A consistency rule caps how much of your total profit is allowed to come from a single trading day. It's the rule most likely to freeze a profitable, rule-abiding account — and the one comparison sites most often get wrong, because the number changes by firm, by plan, and by phase. Here's how it actually works, plus every firm's real figure, checked against their help centers in October 2026.

Here's the trap: you can hit your profit target, break no other rule, and still be unable to withdraw — because one big day made up too much of your total. The consistency rule doesn't take that profit away. It just holds your payout hostage until the rest of your trading "catches up" and your best day is a small enough slice of the whole.

What is a consistency rule?

A consistency rule sets the maximum share of your total profit that any single day is allowed to represent. Say you're on a $50K account with a $3,000 target and a 40% consistency rule. No single day can account for more than 40% of your total profit — so if your total is $3,000, your best day can't exceed $1,200.

Make $2,000 in one heroic session and you've technically hit most of your target, but that day is now 67% of your profit. You're locked until you trade enough additional green days to drag that single day's share back under 40%. The profit is still yours — you just can't touch it yet.

How the consistency rule is calculated

The math is simple: your best day ÷ your total profit ≤ the consistency percentage.

That's why a lower number is stricter, not looser:

  • 50% rule — your best day can be half your total, so you can pass on roughly two balanced days.
  • 40% rule — best day ≤ 40%, so you need about three days.
  • 30% rule — about four days.
  • 20% rule — about five days.
  • 15% rule — roughly seven days of steady profit.

None of this caps how much you can make. It caps how unevenly you can make it.

What is a 20% or 35% consistency rule?

It's the cap expressed as a share of your profit. Under a 20% rule (Lucid Direct, Alpha Direct, Top One Instant Sim Funded) a $3,000 profit allows a best day of $600, so you need at least five solid days. Under a 35% rule (Tradeify Growth once funded) the same $3,000 allows a best day of $1,050, or about three days.

Is it measured since day one or since your last payout?

On most funded accounts, since your last payout: the count starts again after each withdrawal. Lucid Pro checks its 40% rule at payout time and resets it after each payout; Alpha's qualified accounts compare your largest day with net profit since your last request; Apex's 50% rule looks at profit since your last payout. FundedNext's new $50K Direct account is the exception: its 20% "perpetual" rule compares your all-time best day with the current cycle's profit.

Consistency rules by firm

Apex consistency rule (50% on the PA)

Apex has no consistency rule in the evaluation. On the funded Performance Account, no single day may be 50% or more of your profit since your last payout. Going over isn't a breach: the payout just waits until other days dilute it. (Per Apex's help center via search index; Apex blocked our October re-check.)

Tradeify consistency rule

Growth has none in the evaluation and 35% once funded. Select uses 40% in the evaluation (50% with a paid add-on) and none on either funded path, Daily or Flex. Lightning, the instant account, steps up from 20% on the first payout to 25% on the second and 30% from the third (accounts bought after Sep 12, 2025; older ones stay at 20%).

Lucid consistency rule

LucidPro has none in the evaluation (it can be passed in a day) and 40% once funded, checked at payout time and reset after each payout; accounts bought before 11/28/2025 keep 35%. LucidFlex and LucidDaily now use 40% in the evaluation, or 50% with a paid add-on that lets you pass in as few as 2 days, and none once funded. LucidDirect uses 20%.

MFF consistency rule

My Funded Futures applies its rule in the evaluation: 50% on Rapid and Pro, 30% on Rapid EOD. None of the three has a funded consistency rule. Builder is the reverse: no evaluation rule, then 50% at the payout stage.

Alpha Futures consistency rule

Zero has none in the evaluation and 40% once qualified; Standard is 50% in the evaluation and 40% qualified; Advanced is 40% in the evaluation and none qualified; Direct uses 20%. On qualified accounts, going over pauses payouts but doesn't breach the account.

Top One Futures consistency rule

Elite Access has none in the evaluation and 40% once funded. Elite Daily is 40% in the evaluation and none once funded. The instant paths are the strictest: Instant Sim Funded 20% (the trading day ends at 5 PM EST) and Ignite 15%.

FundedNext consistency rule

Flex and Legacy apply 40% in the challenge and none once funded. Rapid Pro is the reverse: none in the challenge, 40% once funded (at least 3 days). Rapid Daily has no rule at any stage, and the new $50K Direct account uses a 20% "perpetual" rule.

Take Profit Trader consistency rule

The TEST requires your best day to stay below 50% of net profit. Once you reach PRO, and then PRO+, there is no consistency rule.

Which plans have no consistency rule?

Among the 27 plans we track, only FundedNext Rapid Daily has no consistency rule at any stage (MFF's small Studio accounts and Top One's limited X-Daily Spark release also have none, but they sit outside our main comparison). Many more drop the rule at one stage, like Take Profit Trader, Tradeify Select and Lucid Flex once funded. The full plan-by-plan list lives at futures prop firms with no consistency rule.

Evaluation vs. funded: when the rule actually applies

This is the detail that trips up most traders and most comparison sites. A firm might advertise "50% consistency" — but is that in the evaluation, the funded account, or both?

  • Evaluation only: Take Profit Trader, Tradeify Select, Lucid's Flex and Daily, MFF Rapid, Rapid EOD and Pro, Alpha Advanced, Top One Elite Daily and FundedNext Flex and Legacy apply the rule to pass the challenge, then drop it once you're funded.
  • Funded only: Apex applies its 50% rule to withdrawals, not to passing. Tradeify Growth (35%), Lucid Pro, Alpha Zero, Top One Elite Access and FundedNext Rapid Pro (all 40%) work the same way.
  • Both, or phase-specific: Alpha Futures' Standard runs 50% in the eval and 40% once qualified; My Funded Futures' Builder has no eval rule but a 50% check at the payout stage.

Always confirm which phase the rule lives in — "no consistency rule" during the funded stage is very different from "no consistency rule" in the evaluation. Our compare tool shows the exact rule for each plan side by side.

How to not break it

  • Bank steady green days instead of swinging for one huge session. The rule punishes lumpy profit, so trade like you're trying to be boring.
  • Know your target and do the math. On a 40% rule with a $3,000 target, keep any single day under ~$1,200.
  • If you have a monster day, keep trading. You don't lose the profit — you just need more days to dilute that day's share below the threshold.
  • Check the phase. If the rule is evaluation-only, you can trade freely once funded; if it's a funded/payout rule, it governs your withdrawals for the life of the account.
  • Read it alongside the payout rules. The consistency rule and the payout process work together — clearing one doesn't mean you've cleared the other.
Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What is a consistency rule in prop trading?+

It's a cap on how much of your total profit can come from a single trading day, usually between 15% and 50%. It's designed to reward steady, repeatable trading over one lucky session, and it determines when your profit becomes withdrawable.

How is the consistency rule calculated?+

Divide your best single day by your total profit. If that number is at or below the firm's consistency percentage, you're compliant. For a 40% rule and $3,000 in profit, your best day must be $1,200 or less.

Is a lower or higher consistency percentage better?+

Higher is easier. A 50% rule lets one day be half your profit, so you can clear it in about two days. A 15% rule forces your profit across roughly seven days. Lower percentages are stricter, not looser.

Which prop firm has the strictest consistency rule?+

Among the firms we track, Top One Futures' Ignite plan is the strictest at 15%, followed by the 20% rules on Top One Instant Sim Funded, Lucid Direct, Alpha Direct and FundedNext's $50K Direct. The looser end sits at 50%.

Do all prop firms have a consistency rule?+

No. FundedNext's Rapid Daily plan has none at any stage, and many plans drop the rule once you're funded (Take Profit Trader, Tradeify Select, Lucid Flex and Daily, MFF Rapid and Pro). Always check the specific plan and phase; the full list is in futures prop firms with no consistency rule.

Do instant-funding accounts have a consistency rule?+

Yes, every instant or straight-to-funded futures plan we track has one: Top One Ignite 15%; Lucid Direct, Alpha Direct, Top One Instant Sim Funded and FundedNext's $50K Direct 20%; Tradeify Lightning 20%, then 25%, then 30% across payouts. Compare them in our instant funding list.

Does the consistency rule reduce my profit?+

No. It never removes profit — it only delays when that profit becomes withdrawable. Once your trading is even enough that no single day exceeds the threshold, the full amount is yours to request.
