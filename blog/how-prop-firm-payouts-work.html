How Do Prop Firm Payouts Work? Rules by Firm

You can request a payout once you clear your firm's gates: qualifying profit days, any consistency rule, a buffer above the drawdown and a minimum request. A cap then limits each withdrawal. Here is each step, with every firm's numbers.

The short version
  • You can't withdraw the moment you're profitable: most plans want qualifying profit days, a buffer above the drawdown and a minimum request ($200 to $1,500) first.
  • Consistency rules decide when profit becomes withdrawable on many plans, and they trip up more traders than any other payout rule.
  • Splits run from 80/20 to 95/5, and Apex pays a flat 100%. A per-payout cap often matters more than the split.
  • How often you can ask ranges from daily (TPT PRO, MFF Rapid, Lucid Daily, Tradeify Select Daily, Top One Elite Daily, FundedNext Rapid Daily) to every 14 days (MFF Pro).
  • The first payout is the hardest. Caps rise and some limits fall away on later payouts.

Passing the evaluation is only half the battle — getting paid is its own process with its own rules. Minimum trading days, consistency requirements, profit splits, and safety-net buffers all sit between you and your first withdrawal. Here's exactly how a futures prop firm payout works, step by step, in 2026.

The reason so many funded traders never see a payout isn't slow banking — it's that they didn't understand the rules gating the withdrawal. A payout is not "I'm profitable, send me money." It's a sequence: get funded, put in the required days, clear the consistency rule, build any required buffer, then request. Miss a step and the money waits.

Step 1: Get funded — sim vs. live

When you pass, most firms fund you in a simulated account first, not the live market. You still get paid real money on your profits, but you're trading in a sim environment the firm mirrors. A few programs (like Take Profit Trader's PRO+ stage) graduate you to a live account after a track record. Sim-funded is standard and not a red flag — it's how the model works.

Step 2: Meet the profit-day requirement

Most plans count qualifying profit days, not calendar days. Apex wants 5 trading days with at least $100–$350 of profit each (by account size), Alpha 5 winning days of $200 or more, Top One Elite Access 5 profitable days of $200–$350, and Tradeify Growth 5 profitable days of $100–$250. The rule stops traders from cashing out one lucky session, and a day you don't trade never counts.

Some plans skip the day count entirely. Take Profit Trader's PRO account pays from day one once the balance clears its buffer, MFF Rapid's first payout can come 24 hours after your first funded trade, Lucid Daily pays daily from profit above a buffer, and Tradeify Lightning and Top One Ignite use a profit target instead of a day count.

Step 3: Clear the consistency rule

A consistency rule caps how much of your profit one day may represent, from 15% (Top One Ignite) to 50% (Apex's funded accounts), so one huge day can hold a payout until smaller green days dilute it. Every plan's number is in our consistency rules by firm guide. To check your own days, use the prop firm consistency calculator.

Step 4: Build the buffer, then request

Many accounts also require a buffer: profit you must build above the starting balance (or above the drawdown) before anything is withdrawable. At Take Profit Trader the PRO buffer equals the max drawdown; at Apex only profit above the drawdown plus $100 can come out. Then there's a minimum request, from $200 on Alpha Zero to $1,000 on Tradeify Lightning, Alpha Advanced and MFF Pro, or $1,500 on a 150K Tradeify Growth or MFF Builder.

Once you're eligible you request, and a cap limits how much each request can take: see payout caps at every futures firm. Then the second clock starts: processing time, which is separate from eligibility. Our ranking of the fastest-payout futures prop firms compares both.

Step 5: Understand your split

Your profit split is how much of the profit you keep. Most futures firms sit at 90/10 (you keep 90%). The range runs from 80/20 (MFF Pro and Builder, Take Profit Trader PRO, FundedNext Legacy) up to 95/5 (FundedNext Flex), and Apex goes further — it now pays a flat 100% of every approved payout, with no split. A higher split matters more the more you scale, so weigh it against the firm's rules, not in isolation.

The safety net (and why the first payout is the hardest)

Many firms apply a safety net or buffer on early payouts: until you've built it, your drawdown may still trail, or only part of your profit is withdrawable. Apex's safety net, for example, is the drawdown plus $100, and caps on Apex, Tradeify Growth and Lucid Pro start low and rise with later payouts. Once you clear the first one or two payouts, those training wheels usually come off — later withdrawals are usually larger and less restricted. The first payout is the gate; after that the process gets easier.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How do prop firm payouts work?+

After you're funded, you trade a (usually simulated) account, meet the plan's profit-day or profit-target requirement, clear any consistency rule and buffer, then request at least the minimum. The firm pays your share of the request, up to the plan's cap, after processing.

How soon can you get your first payout?+

It depends on the plan. Take Profit Trader's PRO account allows withdrawals from day one once the balance clears its buffer; MFF Rapid's first payout can come 24 hours after your first funded trade, and Builder's 48 hours after. Apex, Alpha, Top One Elite Access and Tradeify Growth need 5 qualifying profit days first. Processing then takes anywhere from minutes to a few business days.

What is a good profit split?+

Anything from 90/10 up is strong for futures prop firms. The best go to 95/5, and Apex now pays a flat 100% with no split. Just weigh the split against the firm's drawdown and consistency rules — a great split with harsh rules isn't a great deal.

How do prop firms pay you?+

By bank transfer or a payments platform you set up in the dashboard. The methods firms publish: Take Profit Trader pays via Plaid (US), PayPal or Wise after a review of up to 12 business hours, and charges $50 on wallet withdrawals of $250 or less; Lucid uses Plaid (US), WorkMarket by ADP (bank or PayPal) and crypto for international traders; Alpha Futures pays by ACH, wire/SWIFT, Wise or Rise; Tradeify lists crypto payouts on Growth.

Do prop firms give you a 1099?+

US traders usually get one once a firm's payments to them pass the reporting threshold, which rises to $2,000 for payments made in 2026. The income is taxable either way; our prop firm taxes guide explains which form you'll get and what you owe.
