Apex Trader Funding vs Alpha Futures

Apex sells a one-time evaluation at 90% off and pays you 100% of each approved payout; Alpha bills monthly, pays 90%, and lets Advanced accounts withdraw up to $15,000 at a time with no daily loss limit. Here's how they stack up.

The bottom line

The short version.

Apex Trader Funding wins on entry price: CHAMP takes 90% off the evaluation, a one-time fee with no rebill. Funded PAs keep 100% of each approved payout, every 5 trading days, with a 50% consistency rule and a cap that rises from $1,000 to $2,500 on the first payout to $1,000 to $5,000 by the sixth, after which the PA closes. You choose Intraday Trail (no DLL in the evaluation) or EOD Trail ($500 to $2,000 DLL), and both can pass in a day.

Alpha Futures bills Zero, Standard and Advanced monthly until you pass ($0 activation), and Direct once. It pays 90% from the first withdrawal, up to 4 times a month, each after 5 winning days of $200+ and up to 50% of profit, capped at $1,000 to $2,500 (Zero), $3,000 to $5,000 (Standard) or $15,000 per request (Advanced). Advanced has no daily loss limit at any stage and no news restrictions; Zero passes in a day.

In one line

Want the cheapest one-time entry and 100% payouts? Apex. Want large withdrawals with no DLL or news limits? Alpha Advanced.

  • BillingApex: one-time, no rebill (an evaluation stays active for 30 days). Alpha: monthly until you pass; Direct is one-time.
  • SplitApex: 100% of each approved payout. Alpha: 90% from the first withdrawal.
  • Payout timingApex: every 5 trading days, up to 6 payouts per PA. Alpha: up to 4 withdrawals a month.
  • Biggest withdrawalApex: up to $5,000 (150K, sixth payout). Alpha Advanced: $15,000 per request.
  • Daily loss limitApex Intraday Trail: none in the evaluation (scaling once funded); EOD Trail $500 to $2,000. Alpha Advanced: none; Zero $500 to $2,000.
  • DiscountCHAMP: 90% off at Apex, 40% off at Alpha, in our deal data.

Plan by plan

Plan by plan: Apex vs Alpha Zero, Standard, Advanced and Direct

Apex sells one evaluation in two drawdown styles; Alpha sells four plans. Here's how they line up.

Apex vs Alpha Zero

One-day passes

Neither checks consistency in the evaluation, and from 25K to 100K both use the same targets ($1,500 / $3,000 / $6,000) and drawdowns ($1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000). Funded, Apex checks 50% and Zero 40%. Zero has a DLL at both stages ($500 to $2,000); Apex Intraday Trail has none in the evaluation. First-withdrawal caps: Apex $1,000 / $1,500 / $2,000, Zero $1,000 / $1,500 / $2,500.

Apex vs Alpha Advanced

No daily loss limit

Advanced has no DLL at either stage and no news restrictions, checks 40% in the evaluation and none once qualified, and allows $15,000 per request. It needs at least 3 evaluation days, higher targets ($4,000 / $8,000 / $12,000) and costs $209 to $489 a month. Apex's closest is Intraday Trail: no DLL in the evaluation, but a scaling DLL once funded.

Apex EOD Trail vs Alpha Standard

End-of-day drawdown

Both trail end-of-day. EOD Trail has a DLL in the evaluation ($500 to $2,000) and no evaluation consistency rule; Standard has no DLL until qualified ($1,000 to $3,000) but checks 50% in the evaluation and needs 2 days. Standard caps each withdrawal at $3,000 / $4,000 / $5,000 (50K to 150K) from the first; Apex's caps start lower and rise over 6 payouts.

Apex vs Alpha Direct

No evaluation

Apex has no straight-to-funded plan. Alpha Direct is one-time, $349 to $859 list (40% off with CHAMP), with 20% consistency and caps of $1,000 to $3,000.

Frequently asked

Apex Trader Funding vs Alpha Futures: quick answers.

Which has the bigger discount, Apex or Alpha? +
Apex: 90% off the evaluation with CHAMP, against 40% off at Alpha in our deal data. On a 50K evaluation that works out to $24.90 at Apex (Intraday Trail, Standard) against $83.40 a month at Alpha Zero. Alpha also runs its own public codes; check both on the deals page.
Is Apex or Alpha one-time or monthly? +
Apex is one-time: no rebill, and the evaluation stays active for 30 days. Alpha Zero, Standard and Advanced are monthly until you pass (no monthly fee once qualified); Alpha Direct is one-time.
Which pays more of your profit? +
Apex pays 100% of each approved payout but caps it on a ladder and closes the PA after 6 payouts. Alpha pays 90%, up to 4 times a month, with per-plan caps up to $15,000 on Advanced. Which nets more depends on how much and how often you withdraw.
Which is better for news trading? +
Alpha Advanced, which has no news restrictions. Apex allows trading through news with your normal strategy, but not news-only or straddle strategies or hedging. Alpha Zero, Standard and Direct qualified accounts can hold through news but can't have orders execute within 2 minutes of a red-folder event.
How do the drawdowns compare? +
Apex offers Intraday Trail (no DLL in the evaluation) or EOD Trail (with a DLL), $1,000 to $4,000 by size. Alpha uses an end-of-day trailing max loss limit on every plan; Advanced has no DLL at all. More: Apex trailing drawdown explained.
How many accounts can you run at Apex and Alpha? +
Apex: up to 20 funded PAs across a household, with unlimited evaluations. Alpha: 5 qualified accounts on Zero, Standard or Direct, or 3 if you hold any Advanced account, with one individual per household.
